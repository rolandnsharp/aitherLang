# Speed: what the -O2 tier already gives, and where the time goes

**Status (2026-10-30): measured; nothing changed.** Roland asked whether
to keep optimising C or go back to aitherOS on the metal, and to close
the gap on Python for radio. Before proposing a "tiered compiler" it
turned out aither already has one:

- **Live:** each `send` runs on TCC at once and queues the same C for a
  background `cc -O2 -march=native -ffp-contract=off` build at nice 19,
  swapped in when ready (engine.c, "background -O2 builds"; same layout,
  same bits, since fused multiply-adds are off).
- **Offline:** a render of 4 s or more is built with `cc -O2` first
  (engine.c; render.c's `OPTIMIZE_OVER_SECONDS`). `AITHER_OPT=0` turns
  both off.

So "TCC doesn't optimise" (the earlier handovers) holds only for the
first moments after a send, and for offline renders under 4 s. Several
timings in the 2026-10-29/30 notes were 2 s renders, so TCC: the radio
receiver "just real time at 2.4 MHz" was TCC.

## TCC and -O2, one A72 core (taskset 4), 1.8 GHz

Run time per second of sound, from renders of 6 and 12 s (the
difference, so compiling is taken out), and the start-up it costs:

| patch | TCC | -O2 | faster | -O2 compile |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `pluck_places` (64 places, leapfrog) | 0.70 | 0.033 | 21× | about 2 s |
| `around` (field of modes, a room) | 0.93 | 0.13 | 7× | 2.3 s |
| radio receiver at 2.4 MHz | 0.88 | 0.55 | 1.6× | about 1 s |

`additive_swarm`'s C took gcc 17 s to compile (TCC: instant); live, its
-O2 swap lands that late. The first -O2 build of a session ran slower
than later ones twice (gcc warming its files).

The gain is large where the patch's own arithmetic is the work (fields,
modal banks) and small where natives are (filters, `atan2`), which are
already compiled C.

## Where the receiver's time goes (-O2, per second of 2.4 MHz)

| stage | added |
|---|---|
| the engine and `input()` | 0.117 s |
| tuning: `spin`, `turn` | +0.109 s |
| two `lpf` a side | +0.086 s |
| FM: `phase` (atan2) and `prev` | **+0.230 s** |

`atan2` is 42% of it. `phase` is general (SPEC's frequency of a phasor
is `phase(turn(p, conj(prev(p))))`), and the compiler already has fast
forms for `sin` checked against libm by `make bench`
(c-port-2026-10-28.md). A fast `atan2` within the same tolerance is the
next measurable step. The engine's own floor at 2.4 MHz is 0.16 s a
second (a patch of `0`).

## aitherOS

aitherOS's DESIGN says it is not about throughput (latency, a worst
case, exact replay), and its milestone 0 builds patches' C with gcc -O2
into the kernel. So speed work on the generated C serves both. Its
`emitc` still builds from aitherLang's Nim, which the C port removed;
`aither` can write a patch's C instead, when aitherOS is next worked on.

## The round agreed (2026-10-30), none of it a language change

1. A fast `atan2` like the fast `sin`, checked by `make bench`.
2. Natives visible to gcc: emitted into the generated C, or LTO.
3. Loops instead of unrolling for the -O2 tier (unrolling serves TCC;
   it is why `additive_swarm` takes gcc 17 s).
4. The engine's floor at MHz rates (0.12–0.16 s a second).

Targets proposed, for Roland to set: the 2.4 MHz receiver live on one
A53; `around` live on battery with room; every -O2 swap within 2 s of a
send; the corpus bit-identical.

**Waits, by Roland's request to be reminded once the round below is done:**
blocks, SIMD across samples and multicore. They depend on the last big
design question, the spectral gap: whether a patch can process a block at
a time (FFT, convolution) inside a language that's one sample at a time.
That decision shapes how the compiler schedules work; built first, the
deep machinery may have to be redone.
