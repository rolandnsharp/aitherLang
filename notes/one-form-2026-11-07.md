# One form, seen from above

**Status (2026-11-07): an idea, with its first step built.** Roland: "why couldnt a kick
drum get a simplier form ... isnt there a more general and better way to do
things if we transend and think about this as if looking from above".

Everything a patch models has four parts: **where it lives, its law, what
touches it, and how it is read.** Fields already say these (SPEC "Fields").

- `ring` and `line`, with `ends` and `width`, are where. A sheet would be
  another.
- A filter, a `dho`, a resonator are the same thing on a domain of **one
  place**, with no neighbours. `$x' = $v` could work on plain state, and
  `dho` would be two lines of law. Primes are only on rings today, which is
  an accident of how they came, not a principle.
- A kick is a medium: a head, struck, whose pitch falls because a harder hit
  stretches it tighter. The `500 -> 200 Hz` line in a kick is a description
  of that nonlinearity. As a law it might be stiffness growing with
  displacement.

So the language could get smaller: the domain varies, and the rest stays.

**Where to hold back.** Being able to say it as a law doesn't mean a patch
must. A three-line envelope kick costs nothing and may sound better than a
model, and rhythm and arrangement aren't media. The aim is one form for
physics, not everything as physics.

**To try:** a sheet.

Primes on one place are built (SPEC "Fields"): rates a second, as a place has
no round, in defs and lambdas too. `dho` stays, as it is exact and a law on a
spring runs 11 cents sharp at 3 kHz: the engine's step isn't good enough to
retire it yet.

**Known issue, left for later (Roland, 2026-11-07).** A law on plain state has
its rates a second, so the second is back at one place, against "no time, only
what changes": a place has no round to count in. Giving every oscillator a
period would be awkward where pitch comes from a knob.

**Concentration and slope, tested 2026-11-07.** The wave (`$v' =
-concentration($x)`, `$x' = $v`) and the pair (`$a' = -slope($b)`, `$b' =
-slope($a)`) are the same physics for a plain wave, read `$v` against `$a`:
peaks within 0.3%, shapes within about 1% on lines, about 6% on a ring, and
further apart at 110 Hz over a second (34%, same pitch, different dispersion),
as they step differently. But the wave has `$x` and the pair doesn't, so `held`
or anything nonlinear in x takes a third field: neither replaces the other.
The word is a separate question: a slope of a slope is the same stencil, so
`concentration` went, for `slope(slope($x))`, the textbook's own sign, and the
ring patches render bit for bit as they did. Four slopes are then a bar, with
no new word: `patches/field_bar.aither`. A free end's length turned out as the
wave's, half a place past the last, which put every first mode on its period.

**A kick as a law, 2026-11-07.** One term, `$v' = -$x * w ^ 2 * (1 + stretch *
$x ^ 2) - $v * damp + blow`, gives the sweep with no pitch envelope:
`patches/law_kick.aither`, against the envelope kick, four hits at a time. Hit
hard it starts at 179 Hz, soft at 126, and both fall to its tuning; the
envelope's start at 161 Hz however they're hit. Which sounds better is
Roland's to hear.
