# One patch at three rates: stepping, and what is written in samples

**Status (2026-10-30): audit done, nothing changed in the language or the
stdlib yet.** It answers the question
[stepping-2026-10-29.md](stepping-2026-10-29.md) left open: is step
error a problem in real patches, or only in the gong? It also found a
second thing that matters for running aither at other rates (radio one
day): what is defined in samples rather than in seconds.

## Method

Every tracked patch in `patches/` and `demos/` was rendered for 4.5 s at
44.1, 88.2 and 176.4 kHz (`AITHER_RATE`), except the raster and input
ones (`pixel_index`, `frame_number`, `pixel`, `input()`, `every =`),
whose sound is tied to sample counts by design. 87 were rendered; the
54 that make sound without MIDI were compared. For each render, from
0.5 s on:
- **pitch:** the strongest spectral peak, in cents against the
  176.4 kHz render;
- **shape:** third-octave band levels from 50 Hz to 12.5 kHz, levelled
  against the mean of the bands within 40 dB of the loudest, and
  compared as the RMS dB difference;
- **level:** overall, in dB.

Step error shrinks as the rate rises (a quarter per doubling for a
second-order error). A law written in samples doesn't shrink. So the
two are told apart by comparing 44.1 against 88.2 with 88.2 against
176.4. The measurement floor is 0.00–0.06 dB of shape (sine and pad
patches: `station`, `orbits`, `freq_shifter`, `bass`). The tools are in
[rates/](rates/) (`feat.c`, `judge.py`, `one.sh`), with the full table in
`rates/judged-2026-10-30.txt`.

One mistake was caught on the way. The first version levelled each
spectrum against all 25 bands, including empty ones 100 dB down, which
gave sparse patches a false 1–2 dB, and `field_vdp` a false 7.9 dB.

## Stepping: only one patch is hurt

The stdlib's oscillators, alone, cents off the true frequency:

| | 220 Hz | 1 kHz | 3 kHz | 6 kHz |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `resonator` (`dho`: the exact turn) | +0.2 | −0.1 | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| `tuning_fork` (semi-implicit Euler) | +0.3 | +1.6 | +13.5 | +56.7 |
| `vdp` (the same step; against its own 176.4 kHz render) | +2.1 | +10.7 | +41.3 | |

These are at 44.1 kHz. At 88.2 and 176.4 kHz the error quarters with
each doubling, and `tuning_fork`'s matches the textbook figure for its
step (13.3 cents predicted at 3 kHz). `bowed_string` and `struck_bar`
step every partial the same way, so a struck bar at 440 Hz has its top
partial (13.3×) about 55 cents sharp.

In the corpus:
- **`chaos`** is the one patch with real step error. A Lorenz system
  stepped by hand with forward Euler, time scaled 55×, has its main peak
  21 cents sharp at 44.1 kHz and 1 cent at 88.2 kHz.
- **`vdp`** is used by `aeolian` and `field_vdp` at pitches where its
  error is about 1 cent.
- **`tuning_fork`, `bowed_string` and `struck_bar` are used by no
  patch.**
- Every coupled field in the corpus (`sync`, `field_ring`,
  `field_coupled`, `phasor_ring`, `knot`) is at the floor for pitch and
  shape. `phasor_ring`'s level moves, but that is its coupling written
  per sample (below), not its step.

**So the stepping question doesn't earn a construct.** In the corpus,
step error hurts one hand-written chaotic system and three unused stdlib
defs. The gong, which ran away, was a coupled nonlinear field at a
coupling of 100, and it has been dropped. The remedies are local:
- the three unused defs built on `resonator` (exact), or removed (the
  default is no);
- `chaos` stepped by a second-order step by hand, or by a small def if
  a second patch wants the same;
- `vdp`'s linear part turned exactly as `dho` turns, with the
  nonlinearity as the push, if `vdp` is ever wanted high.

`ab2` (Adams–Bashforth as a def, 1.5 of the push less 0.5 of the last)
stays the candidate for a coupled field that needs more than a turn. It
waits for one.

## What is written in samples

Differences that don't shrink as the rate rises, and what causes them:

| written in samples | evidence | what it does at another rate |
|---|---|---|
| `impulse()`: one sample, height 1 | `dho_metamorphosis`: +7.4 and +4.6 dB per doubling (6 dB expected) | a strike made from it is weaker as the rate rises |
| `noise()`: a fixed size per sample | `flute`: −2.9 and −3.7 dB per doubling (3 dB expected: energy per Hz) | breath against tone shifts; noise-driven peaks move |
| `reverb`: comb and allpass lengths in samples (1557, 1617, 1491, 1422; 225, 556: Schroeder's figures for 44.1 kHz), and a damping of 0.3 a sample | `three_phase` (reverb only): 2.3 and 2.2 dB of shape | the decay time is kept (from `sr`), but the room is 4× smaller at 176.4 kHz and its damping moves up |
| a coupling or step "per sample" in a patch | `phasor_ring`: level +0.6, −2.4 dB | the law itself changes with the rate |
| `hilbert` (inside `analytic`, `freq_shift`): fixed allpass coefficients | not measured | its working band is a fraction of the rate: clean above about 30 Hz at 48 kHz, above about 1.5 kHz at 2.4 MHz |

These already matter today: the corpus renders at 48 kHz and Roland
plays at 44.1 kHz. For radio (SDR rates in MHz) they are the work. The
port from Nim to C hard-codes no rate: `sr` and `dt` are read as the
patch runs, and the compiler uses the rate only to size buffers.

## A tie, fixed

`bell` skipped a strike at 176.4 kHz (−43 dB where the other rates had
−22). It chose which bell to strike with `int(phasor(rate / 8) * 8)` at
the same instant `impulse(rate)` fired, and both clocks cross a whole
number at exactly that moment. Which side of it a sample fell on was
rounding in each clock's running sum. Reading the count half a step
off (`+ 0.5`, `mod 8`) brings the strike back and is bit-identical at
44.1, 48 and 88.2 kHz.

## Next

- Decide the per-sample builtins. `impulse` with unit area (height `sr`)
  and `noise` with a fixed energy per Hz would make strikes and breath
  the same at any rate, but they change the sound of every patch that
  uses them, at 48 kHz too, unless they are scaled to be equal there.
  `reverb` in seconds is the same question.
- The three unused stdlib oscillators: rebuilt on `resonator`, or
  removed.
- `chaos`: a second-order step by hand.
