# Spinors, 3-D fields, hearing height, and time without t

**Status (2026-11-05): a design note, nothing built.** Written to keep
an idea and its open choices before any code: what 3-D fields need, why
the answer is spin and not matrices, the one real gap it shows in the
language (no pair of pairs), what hearing height needs (pinnae,
convolution), and how physics thinks about time other than as `t`.
Roland's two corrections drive it: field theorists don't compute with
matrices, they use spin; and they don't think in terms of `t`.

## How it came up

`patches/around.aither` holds the sound field round a listener in the
plane of the ears as circular modes, each a pair: a source is a turn into
every mode, a turning head is a turn of every mode, hearing is a sum of
turns ([spatial-2026-10-29.md](spatial-2026-10-29.md)). Item 5 there said
3-D is harder: turning about the vertical axis is still a turn of each m,
but pitch and roll "mix the m's within each l (Wigner matrices), which a
turn doesn't say". That is true and the long way round. Physics doesn't
build rotations from those matrices; it builds them, and the matrices,
from spin.

## Spinors in a page

**A direction is a spinor.** Two complex numbers, for a direction θ from
the pole and φ round it:

    ξ = cos(θ/2)        η = e^(iφ) sin(θ/2)

and back again (the Hopf map): x + iy = 2 conj(ξ) η, z = |ξ|² − |η|².

**A rotation is two complex numbers**, a and b with |a|² + |b|² = 1 (the
Cayley–Klein parameters; a unit quaternion; the group SU(2)). It acts on
a spinor by complex multiplication and conjugation alone:

    ξ' = a ξ + b η          η' = −conj(b) ξ + conj(a) η

Two rotations compose by multiplying their (a, b) as quaternions. Any
rotation, yaw, pitch and roll together, is one (a, b); a head is one
(a, b) that changes with time.

**It is a double cover**, and that is physics, not an inconvenience:
(a, b) and (−a, −b) turn every direction the same way, but a spinor
turned through 360° comes back negated, and only after 720° as it was.
Spin-½ fields (the electron's) behave exactly so. A pair (aither's
complex number) can't: a turn by 2π is the identity on a pair.

**Higher modes are products of spinors.** A spin-l object has 2l + 1
components, and they turn as the symmetric products of 2l spinors: the
components ∝ √C(2l, l+m) · ξ^(l+m) η^(l−m) for a "beam" pointing along
(ξ, η), the spin-l coherent state (Schwinger's oscillator construction of
angular momentum; Majorana's stellar representation, a spin-l state as
2l points on the sphere, here all 2l at the source). To turn the whole
field you turn the one spinor and multiply again; the Wigner matrices are
those products expanded, never stored.

A caution for the audio use, to be worked out before code: ambisonics
encodes a direction with the spherical harmonics Y_lm, which are the
rotated m = 0 state and need conj(ξ), conj(η) as well; the coherent
state above is the rotated m = l state and needs only ξ, η. Both turn
correctly under rotation, and their spatial "beams" differ (the coherent
state's response falls off as cos^(2l)(γ/2) with the angle γ from the
source). Which reads better as a sound field is a measurement, as
around's were.

References: Penrose and Rindler, *Spinors and Space-Time*, Cambridge
1986, https://doi.org/10.1017/cbo9780511524486 (two-spinor calculus,
the Lorentz group as SL(2, C)); Schwinger's oscillator method as
presented in Mejía and Pleitez, Rev. Bras. Ens. Fís. 24, 2002,
https://doi.org/10.1590/s1806-11172002000100007; Majorana's stellar
representation as used in Liu and Fu, Phys. Rev. A 94, 2016,
https://doi.org/10.1103/physreva.94.022123. (Majorana 1932 and Schwinger
1952 themselves weren't in the index searched; cite them once read.)

## In aither's terms

What it needs, aither has: `turn` (complex multiplication), `conj`, `+`,
and powers by repeated turns (`turn(p, p)` is how octave stacks are
already written). Encoding a source, turning a head, and hearing from a
direction are all turns, conjugates and sums. That is the
[PHASORS.md](../PHASORS.md) picture carried one dimension up.

**The gap: a spinor is two pairs, and so is a rotation.** aither's values
are numbers and pairs (a pair is a complex number, and a pair of pairs is
an error by design; see the "two channels, complex first" decision). A
def returns one value, so it can't return a spinor or a quaternion. Three
ways, not chosen:

1. **Leave the language alone.** Carry a spinor as two `let`s and write
   the rotation out where it's used, or as two defs (one for ξ', one for
   η'), each called. Nothing changes; the maths is visible; it reads as
   twice the text and repeats itself.
2. **Let a def take and return a small array of pairs**, so `spinor(θ, φ)`,
   `qturn(q, s)` and `qmul(q1, q2)` read like the maths. Arrays of pairs
   already exist (local and state arrays, `pairs`); what is new is one
   as a def's value. This is a case for the arrays brief
   ([arrays-2026-10-30.md](arrays-2026-10-30.md)), not a new kind of
   value, and a field of spinors (a Weyl field: one spinor a place)
   would be a state array of them.
3. **A quaternion or spinor as a first-class value.** Most direct for the
   maths; widens the pair idea itself (a value wider than two), which
   the language has refused so far. Not recommended without a long run
   of patches that need it.

Suggested order: write a 3-D field patch in way 1 (a few sources placed
as spinor products, the field turned by a head quaternion, heard
through two ears), measure it as around was measured, and read how badly
it reads. That is the brief for way 2.

## Why it matters beyond games

For game audio the plane of the ears covers most of what is heard, and
spinors may be more than it needs. For aither as a place to model real
fields (Roland's research), they are a real gap:

- **Spin-½ fields are spinor-valued.** Pairs model spin-0 (a complex
  scalar field: a phase and a size at every place) and rotations in a
  plane. Electrons, neutrinos, the Dirac and Weyl equations need a spinor
  at every place, and their 720° periodicity can't be faked with pairs.
- **The same two complex numbers carry relativity.** Drop the condition
  |a|² + |b|² = 1 and keep a d − b c = 1 for the 2×2 matrix [[a, b],
  [c, d]]: the group is SL(2, C), the double cover of the Lorentz group.
  Rotations are its unitary part; boosts are rotations by imaginary
  angles (Penrose and Rindler). So spinors are the step from aither's
  plane rotations to relativistic fields, with nothing but complex
  multiplication.

## Hearing height: pinnae and convolution

The maths of 3-D is clean with spin; hearing it isn't, and that is the
ears. Height is heard mostly through the outer ear, whose folds colour
sound with notches that move as a source rises and falls. Two headphone
channels through a sphere (around's head) carry no such colour, so
elevation is mostly inaudible. Two ways to give it:

- **A structural pinna model**: Brown and Duda model the pinna as a few
  echoes whose delays depend on elevation and azimuth, beside the head's
  shadow and delay ("A structural model for binaural sound synthesis",
  IEEE Trans. Speech Audio Process. 6, 1998,
  https://doi.org/10.1109/89.709673). A few `delay`s per ear: aither-native,
  physics, cheap, and the first thing to try.
- **Measured heads**: a head-related transfer function per direction and
  ear, a few hundred filter taps each (the CIPIC database: 45 subjects,
  Algazi, Duda, Thompson and Avendano, IEEE WASPAA 2001,
  https://doi.org/10.1109/aspaa.2001.969552). That needs convolution,
  which aither doesn't have (spatial-2026-10-29, item 4). Convolution is
  its own design question, and would also bring measured rooms and
  instrument bodies.

On the uConsole's speakers, a few centimetres apart, none of this is
heard; binaural is for headphones.

## Time without t

Physics seldom writes a field as "its value now, stepped to the next
now". aither's base is a sample at a time, because that is what a sound
card asks for, but several of physics' framings are already in it, and
the rest are questions. Textbook-standard; no references checked here.

1. **Time as rotation.** A mode's whole time-dependence is a phase,
   e^(−iωt): you write the mode, its frequency and its turn, and a
   stationary state changes only by turning. aither's `spin`, `phasor`
   and `dho` treat time this way ([PHASORS.md](../PHASORS.md)), and
   rain's "how long since" is time read off a clock's phase, not counted
   (COMPOSING, "How long since").
2. **Time generated by the law.** A state evolves by an operator the law
   generates, exp(−iHt); for one step it is a turn. Every `turn($z,
   step)` per sample in aither is that operator. Writing the law apart
   from its stepping ([stepping-2026-10-29.md](stepping-2026-10-29.md))
   is the same idea from the other side.
3. **Time as one coordinate of spacetime.** There is no shared now; a
   boost mixes time and space; each observer carries proper time. aither
   already has two clocks, `t` shared by every voice and `sweep` (or a
   `$proper` the voice integrates) its own: coordinate and proper time
   (COMPOSING, "The composition clock"). Spinors under SL(2, C) are how
   boosts would enter.
4. **Imaginary time.** Replace t by −iτ and oscillation becomes decay,
   waves become diffusion. A step that turns and shrinks, as `dho`'s does,
   is a mixture of the two: damping is a rotation partly into imaginary
   time.
5. **Whole histories.** Least action and the path integral choose or sum
   over a field's entire history at once; time order isn't the starting
   point. In aither, history is a thing in `tape` and `delay`, where a
   read head can be anywhere along it, and an offline render evaluates a
   span.
6. **Relational time.** No outside clock: time is one part of a system
   read against another. A patch can run from a clock it builds (a
   `phasor`, a `vdp`) instead of `t`, and the engine's `sweep` is set from
   outside, as an oscilloscope's trigger is.

The open question these leave for the language: whether a patch could be
written as a law over space and time together (a field equation with
both on the same footing) and stepped by the engine, rather than as the
per-sample code that steps it. That is stepping-2026-10-29's question in
its widest form.
