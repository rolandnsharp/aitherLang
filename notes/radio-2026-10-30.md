# Radio: a receiver at 2.4 MHz, and where the rate changes

**Status (2026-10-30): a receiver works on a synthetic antenna; the rate
changes at the end of the pipe, in `tools/rate` (built).** No SDR is attached (the
uConsole's USB bus has only its hubs, the LAN adapter and the keyboard),
so the antenna was made by aither too. The earlier note
([listening-2026-10-28.md](listening-2026-10-28.md), "The one real gap")
named the problem: "An SDR gives MHz, a patch runs at one rate, and
decimation has no primitive yet."

## The test

A transmitter patch, rendered at 2.4 MHz as IQ (a pair, I and Q):

```
let a = spin(200000 + 75000 * 0.5 * sin(TAU * phasor(1000)))          # a station 200 kHz up, a 1 kHz tone
let b = spin(-300000 + 75000 * 0.5 * sin(TAU * phasor(440))) * 0.7    # another 300 kHz down, 440 Hz
(a + b) * 0.3 + [noise(), noise()] * 0.01
```

A receiver, reading it through `input()`:

```
let iq = input()
let tuned = turn(iq, conj(spin(200000)))                       # the station turned down to 0
let ch = lpf(lpf(tuned, 100000, 0.6), 100000, 0.6)             # only its channel
let speed = phase(turn(ch, conj(prev(ch)))) / (TAU * dt)       # how fast it turns: Hz off the carrier
speed / 37500 * 0.5
```

FM demodulation is SPEC's own rule for a phasor's frequency ("Read the
whole pair"); tuning is a turn by a conjugate spin. Nothing new.

Output reduced to 48 kHz outside aither (a boxcar of 50) and measured
from 0.2 s on:

| | sent | received |
|---|---|---|
| 1 kHz (this station) | 0.5 | 0.4997, all of the output to 0.0 dB |
| 440 Hz (the other station) | — | 105 dB down |

## Cost

2 s of the receiver at 2.4 MHz: 1.9 s on an A72 at 1.8 GHz (just faster
than real time), 5.1 s on an A53 at 1.4 GHz (2.6× too slow). Those were
TCC (a 2 s render isn't built with -O2). With the -O2 build it runs at
0.55 s a second on the A72, and 42% of that is `atan2`
([perf-2026-10-30.md](perf-2026-10-30.md)). So 2.4 MHz
is live only on mains and only just. One broadcast FM station needs about
200 kHz of band; an RTL-SDR runs from about 225 kHz, so a receiver at
240–300 kHz costs a tenth and fits on battery. 2.4 MHz is for seeing a
wide band at once (a waterfall), not for listening to one station.

## Where the rate changes

The receiver's sound is at the SDR's rate; the speaker wants 48 kHz.
Something has to change the rate, and aither's model is one rate a
program ("a program is one sample at one place"). The options:

1. **At the ends, by a tool.** `rtl_sdr … | aither rx.aither | tools/rate
   48000 | tools/play`: the receiver runs at the radio's rate, a tool
   low-passes and decimates, and anything after it (effects, a mix)
   runs in a second aither at 48 kHz: `… | tools/rate 48000 | aither
   fx.aither | tools/play`. It is SPEC's rule for what a tool is:
   "programs are the ends, functions are the middle", and the pair
   stream already means whatever its reader says (pair-output-2026-10-15.md).
   Nothing in the language changes.
2. **Inside a patch, a slower part.** A part stepped every N samples
   (stepped arrays' `every` is the shape). That is a control rate under
   another name: a part of the patch that runs less often than the
   sample. It is the machine the house rule keeps out of patches.
3. **Inside a patch, a rate per voice.** The engine runs voices at
   different rates and converts between them where they meet. One
   engine, but every voice and mix point acquires a rate, and the
   engine a resampler.

**Recommendation: 1.** It keeps one rate a program, needs no language
change, and composes as the rest of aither does, through the pipe. Its
cost is a tool (`tools/rate`: a windowed-sinc or two-stage half-band
decimator, rational ratios for 2.4 MHz → 48 kHz = 50:1) and a second
engine when there is audio work after the receiver. The receiver itself
pays the radio's rate for everything in it, which is right: everything
in it is radio.

## Decided: one engine at the radio's rate, the rate changed at the end

Roland asked whether a tool hides logic from the patch, out of reach of
live playing. What the tool holds has no choice in it: drop samples
behind a fixed anti-alias filter, as a sound card does at its edge.
Tuning, the channel, the demodulator, de-emphasis, squelch, any AGC or
AFC and the audio band all stay in the patch. What would hide logic is
two engines, a receiver and then a second aither for effects at 48 kHz:
one instrument split into two programs on two sockets. So: **one engine
at the radio's rate, everything in the receiver patch, the rate changed
only at the end**: `radio | aither rx | tools/rate | tools/play`. At
240–300 kHz an audio effect costs 5–6 times what it does at 48 kHz.

## Why the filter can't be left out

Keeping one sample in 50 folds everything above the new Nyquist (24 kHz)
into the audio: 38 kHz comes out at 10 kHz. Above 24 kHz a receiver's
output holds a real station's stereo signal (23–53 kHz) and RDS (57
kHz), and FM's noise, which rises with frequency, all the way to 1.2
MHz. The receiver's output above, taken to 48 kHz three ways, with what
is left after the 1 kHz tone:

| | left over, below the tone |
|---|---|
| every 50th sample, no filter | 15.1 dB |
| the mean of each 50 (a crude filter) | 33.0 dB |
| a 1001-tap windowed sinc at 20 kHz | 39.3 dB |
| `tools/rate` | 41.1 dB |

The patch's own `lpf` is two poles, only a few dB down at 24 kHz, and
running a long filter inside the patch would compute every one of the
2.4 million samples a second; at the rate change it computes only the
48,000 kept.

## tools/rate (built)

`tools/rate FROM TO`: float32 pairs in at FROM, out at TO, either way
(down for a receiver, up for a transmitter fed from 48 kHz). The ratio
is reduced to L/M; one Blackman-windowed sinc passes 0.4 of the lower
rate and stops by 0.5 of it; each output is computed from its own
phase's taps. Both rates are arguments, since `AITHER_RATE` is set for
the whole shell and the tool sits between two rates.

| test | result |
|---|---|
| 1 kHz and 18 kHz, 2.4 MHz → 48 kHz | 0.500001 and 0.500081 of 0.5 |
| 30 kHz (would fold to 18 kHz) | 0.000003, 104 dB down |
| 100 kHz (would fold to 4 kHz) | nothing measurable |
| 44.1 → 48 kHz, 1 kHz | exact; the rest 123 dB below |
| 48 kHz → 2.4 MHz, 1 kHz | exact; the rest 119 dB below |

Cost for 2 s: 2.4 MHz → 48 kHz in 0.8 s on an A72, 2.4 s on an A53 at
1.4 GHz (just short of real time); 240 kHz → 44.1 kHz in 0.3 s on an
A53. A two-stage filter would make the 2.4 MHz case cheaper if it is
ever played on battery.
