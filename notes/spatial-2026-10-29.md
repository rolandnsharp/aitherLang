# Spatial audio as a field of modes, by hand

**Status (2026-10-29): worked through and measured in today's aither;
no language change made.** Step 1 of the handover's "Where to start"
([handover-fields-2026-10-29.md](handover-fields-2026-10-29.md)). The
patch is `patches/around.aither`. Design first: nothing below is built
into the language.

## The representation

Only the plane of the ears, for now. The field round a listener, seen
from the centre of the head, is a function of direction θ, and in modes
it is

    p(θ) = a_0 + 2 Σ_{m≥1} Re(a_m · e^{-imθ})

Each mode a_m is one pair, so the field is a pair array, `array(order + 1,
[0, 0])`. Mode 0 is how much sound there is from everywhere; mode m is how
much of it winds m times round the head, and its phase says where it points.
This is 2-D ambisonics (circular harmonics). It is M as the handover put
it: magnitude and phase, no coordinates, with space entering only where
the field is heard.

| step | the physics | in aither today |
|---|---|---|
| a source s at θ | projected into every mode: a_m += s · e^{imθ} | `a[m] = s * toward(m, theta)`, with `toward(m, θ) = rotate([1, 0], m * θ)` |
| the head turns by ψ | the field turns the other way: mode m by −mψ | `a[m] = rotate(a[m], -m * yaw)` |
| heard from direction φ | `at`: each mode turned back by mφ and its real side taken, weighted (max-rE, g_m = cos(mπ / 2(M+1))) | `heard(a, phi)`: a `sum` of turns |
| to two ears | eight fixed directions round the head, each through a round head to each ear | `ears(a, K)`: a `sum` of `ear`s |

The head is Brown and Duda's (1998) sphere. For one ear and a sound at
angle θ from the ear's axis:
- **The delay** is Woodworth's path round the sphere, (a/c)(1 − cos θ) on
  the near side and (a/c)(1 + θ − π/2) on the far side.
- **The shadow** is one shelf, α·x + (1 − α)·lp1(x, c/(πa)), with
  α = 1.05 + 0.95 cos(1.2θ). That is up 6 dB facing the ear and down past
  1.2 kHz behind the head.

Both are constant for each of the eight directions, because the
directions are fixed in the head and only the modes turn.

The vocabulary is the gong's. Encoding a source is `kick`, a point
push resolved into the modes, with e^{imθ} as each mode's shape at that
place. Hearing from a direction is `at`. And a turning head is `spin`:
turning at rate Ω makes mode m spin at mΩ, a dispersion ω(m) = mΩ. A
plate's modes and the air's modes round a head are held, pushed and
observed the same way. The only difference is that spatial modes carry
a signal rather than ringing by themselves.

## The patch, today's form

```
def toward(m, theta): rotate([1, 0], m * theta)

def heard(a, phi):
  let n = len(a)
  sum(n, k =>
    let m = k - 1
    let [re, _] = turn(a[m], toward(-m, phi))
    re * (if m == 0 then 1 else 2 * cos(m * PI / (2 * n)))) / K

def ear(p, lo, theta):
  let th = abs(theta)
  let alpha = 1.05 + 0.95 * cos(th * 1.2)
  let path = if th < PI / 2 then 1 - cos(th) else 1 + th - PI / 2
  delay(alpha * p + (1 - alpha) * lo, head / c * path, 0.002)

def ears(a, K):
  let both = sum(K, j =>
    let phi = TAU * (j - 1) / K
    let p = heard(a, phi)
    let lo = lp1(p, c / head / PI)
    [ear(p, lo, wrap_angle(phi - PI / 2)), ear(p, lo, wrap_angle(phi + PI / 2))])
  let dull = lp1(both, 1500)
  dull + 1.6 * (both - dull)          # the treble the K directions lose (see "Judged by analysis")

let a = array(n, [0, 0])
each(n, m =>
  a[m] = shaker * toward(m, a_shaker) + bell * toward(m, a_bell) + drone * toward(m, a_drone)
  a[m] = rotate(a[m], -m * yaw))
ears(a, K)
```

A shaker circles every 7 s, a bell sits behind on the left and a drone
ahead on the right. The gamepad's A stick turns the head.

## Measured

All on mains with every core on. One white-noise source at a fixed
azimuth, rendered 0.6 s, through the field at orders 1–3 (K = 8
directions) and, as the reference, straight through the same head at
its own direction with no modes (an "object"). ILD is broadband; ITD is
the cross-correlation peak below 1 kHz. Positive values mean louder or
earlier on the left. From `ears.c` in the session's scratchpad (not
kept); the test patch is `around.aither`'s defs with one source.

| azimuth | object ILD, ITD | order 1 | order 2 | order 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15° | 3.7 dB, 165 µs | 3.6 dB, 80 µs | 2.1 dB, 175 µs | 3.7 dB, 181 µs |
| 30° | 7.3, 328 | 6.5, 384 | 5.1, 317 | 5.8, 342 |
| 45° | 10.4, 474 | 8.0, 458 | 8.5, 490 | 8.0, 485 |
| 60° | 11.0, 594 | 8.5, 490 | 10.4, 582 | 10.7, 605 |
| 90° | 13.0, 744 | 8.5, 511 | 10.7, 631 | 11.4, 679 |

- **Turning the field is exact.** A source at 1.0 rad with the head at
  0.4 rad and a source at 0.6 rad with the head at 0 are equal sample for
  sample (largest difference 0.0). Each mode turned by m times the angle
  is the scene turned.
- **Few modes blur the sides.** At 90°, order 1 gives 69% of the
  object's ITD and 4.5 dB less ILD. Order 3 gives 91% and 1.6 dB less.
  This is the physics of truncation: order M holds the field across a
  head of radius a up to about kr = M, which is f ≈ M · c / (2πa) ≈
  M × 620 Hz. Beyond that the modes cannot carry the difference between
  the ears, and the decode spreads the sound over neighbouring
  directions. At low order the curve is also lumpy (order 1 at 15°:
  80 µs against 165), which is the eight directions showing through.
- **Front and back are the same.** 15° and 165° measure identically: a
  sphere with ears at exactly ±90° is symmetric front to back. Brown
  and Duda put the ears about 10° back and add a pinna echo. The real
  cure is moving the head, which the stick does: a source ahead moves
  the other way from one behind as you turn.
- **Cost.** 20 s of the patch renders in 5.5 s on one A53 at 1.4 GHz
  (2.3 s on an A72): about 3.6× real time on a small core, so it should
  hold on battery (four A53s at 1 GHz; not timed on battery). Orders
  1, 2 and 3 take 4.3, 5.1 and 5.8 s. Most of the cost is fixed: the
  eight directions' 16 delays and 8 shelves, which don't depend on how
  many sources there are.

## Judged by analysis, as a listener would

These are the questions listening would answer, put to the output
through a simple model of the ears. Each ear's signal is split into
third-octave bands (250 Hz to 12.5 kHz). The time difference between
the ears is read below 1 kHz and the level difference above 2 kHz, as
hearing uses them. The angle a listener would hear is the true
direction whose cues (rendered straight through the head) match. The
reference is the same sphere, so these numbers judge the modes, not
the head.

**Where it is heard.** From the time difference (250–1000 Hz), as RMS
error over 0–90° in 5° steps: order 1 is 19°, order 2 is 9°, order 3
is 6°. Order 3 is within 2° up to 60° and pulled in beyond that (90°
is heard at 72°). Order 1 piles everything from 45° to 135° at about
45°. The level difference (2–8 kHz, mean in dB) can't be turned into
an angle, because the sphere's own curve peaks near 60° and comes back
down. Read directly:

| azimuth | object | order 1 | order 2 | order 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30° | 8.6 | 7.3 | 5.7 | 6.0 |
| 60° | 13.1 | 9.5 | 11.8 | 12.0 |
| 90° | 12.2 | 9.2 | 11.7 | 10.7 |

**Timbre: the field was dull, and a shelf mends it.** Averaged over
azimuth, order 3 came out 2 dB down at 1 kHz, 4–5 dB down at 2–5 kHz
and 6 dB down at 8 kHz compared with the sound sent straight through
the head. Order 1 was 5–9 dB down. The eight directions reach each ear
at different delays: below about 1 kHz they add in phase, above it
with random phases. This is the known colouring of a binaural
ambisonic decode. A fixed shelf on the two ears, `lo + 1.6 * (x - lo)`
with `lo = lp1(x, 1500)`, brings order 3 within 2 dB of the true
direction from 250 Hz to 8 kHz (+2.2 dB at 12.5 kHz). It is now in
`ears`. What a shelf can't mend is the swing: as a source moves, a
band's colour changes by 3.8 dB on average (worst 7.9 dB) at order 3,
as it passes from one of the eight directions to the next.

**A moving source.** One turn every 7 s, with the time difference read
in 0.1 s windows over half a turn (with the shelf):

| | wobble (RMS second difference) | RMS off the true path |
|---|---|---|
| object | 7 µs | — |
| order 1 | 54 µs | 134 µs |
| order 3 | 22 µs | 29 µs (about 3° near the front) |

Order 3 tracks. Order 1 lurches: from 8° to 23° its time difference
overshoots (21 to 330 µs, against 85 to 262), then it sticks at about
500 µs from 54° to 131° while the true value rises to 750 and comes
back.

**What this can't judge.** Whether it sounds outside the head, how
the sphere compares with a real head (front and back, elevation, the
pinnae), and whether the patch is music. The first two could be
measured against a measured head (a public HRTF set such as CIPIC or
MIT's KEMAR) offline, with no listening. The third is Roland's.

## A room, after listening

Roland's first listen of the dry patch, on headphones: inside the head,
moving ear to ear. That is expected without a room. Reflections and a
reverberant tail that differs at the two ears are the strongest cue
that a sound is outside, and the sphere has no pinnae.

So the patch now has a 6 × 4.5 × 2.8 m room. Each source arrives by 7
paths: straight, off the four walls (image sources), and off the floor
and ceiling. The floor and ceiling paths come from the source's own
bearing, because the field holds only the plane of the ears. Each path
is a `delay` of its length, weakened by 1/r and by a wall's 0.7. That
is 21 arrivals put into the field, and the decode costs what it did
for 3: the field's strength, used. The late tail is a reverb fed from
the field's left and right beams. Its treble-weighted coherence
between the ears is −0.21, so it already differs at the two ears
without a second decorrelator. The ears moved to ±100°, as Brown and
Duda put them.

- **Cost.** Each arrival's direction is kept as a unit pair, and mode m
  reached by turning it by itself (`wound`). That removed 84 cos/sin
  pairs and an atan2 per sample, gave the same output to 8e-8, and took
  20 s of output from 27.6 s to 14.6 s on one A53 at 1.4 GHz (5.9 s on
  an A72). At 1 GHz that is about real time on one core, so it is not
  yet safe on battery. Not timed on battery.
- **Heard with the room** (Roland, headphones): "much closer to something running around my head." The room was the main missing cue, as expected.

## Front and back: where listening stopped

The listens after the room, in order, all on headphones:
- **The turn didn't work at first**, for two reasons. The control was on
  CC 20, A's up/down, and three pad processes were fighting over the
  keyboard grab. Then it could turn only left, which carries the bell
  from 137° to 47°, two directions with the same cues on a sphere. It
  is now CC 21, with d-pad right turning the head right, 17° a press.
- **Turned:** "still the same basically. maybe it passes slightly behind
  my head on one pass but not really in front, it does seem to go
  beyond my left and right ears though." So left and right are now
  outside the head. Front and back are not.
- **An outer-ear shadow** (each direction loses treble above 3 kHz in
  proportion to how far behind it is, `pinna = 1`) made straight behind
  4–5 dB duller than straight ahead at 2–8 kHz and 10 dB duller at
  12.5 kHz, against exactly 0 without it. Heard: no difference.

That is what the literature leads one to expect. With static, generic
cues on headphones, front/back confusion stays common even with
measured (non-individual) HRTFs. What fixes it is head movement the
listener makes with their own head, or their own ears' measurements.
Neither is available here, since the uConsole has no head tracker.
More tuning of the sphere is unlikely to be heard. The perceptual
work stops at "outside the head, left and right; front and back
ambiguous", and the remaining questions are the language's.

## Objects and the field: the same split as M and X

The table above says when to use which:
- **The direct sound of a few sources is best as objects.** Each source
  goes through the head at its own direction, which is exact at any
  frequency and costs two ears per source. That is X's side: a place,
  observed.
- **The field is for everything else.** Many sources (the ear cost is
  fixed), turning the scene (one turn per mode), reverb and ambience
  (diffuse, so blur costs nothing), and a recorded ambisonic field
  played back.

With eight directions, a field is cheaper than objects past about eight
sources. A patch can mix both, objects for what is near and the field
for the room, and add the two ear pairs.

## What reads badly: the brief

1. **A file constant in a def isn't compile-time for `sum`.** `ears`
   wanted `sum(K, …)` with `K` the file's `let K = 8`. The compiler
   refused it ("sum: first arg must be a compile-time-constant integer"),
   although the def may read `K` and SPEC says a name bound to a
   compile-time number is accepted. Passing `K` in works, and `heard`
   still divides by the file's `K`, so the two defs now disagree. Whether a
   file constant counts as compile-time inside a def is a rule to decide
   against SPEC's scope rule for defs, not a gap to loosen quietly.
2. **A stereo source in a mode is silently wrong.** `osc(saw, [98, 98.4])
   * toward(m, θ)` compiles and gives `[l · cos mθ, r · sin mθ]`. A pair
   is both a stereo signal and a mode's complex number, and the product of
   a stereo signal and a mode needs a pair of pairs, which aither doesn't
   have. Today a source has to be mono. Refusing it may not be possible
   today: the compiler can't tell a stereo pair from a phasor, because
   both are just pairs. It is the same double meaning that V's `{a, b}`
   was meant to remove ([fields-2026-10-29.md](fields-2026-10-29.md)),
   and it needs its own design, not a quick error.
3. **The field can't be made by a def.** `heard` and `ears` take an
   array and return a pair, so they could go in the stdlib today. Encoding
   and turning can't: a def can't return an array, so the patch declares
   `let a = array(n, [0, 0])` and fills it with `each`. Two lines isn't
   much. What would help is tabulation, `array(n, m => …)`, a local array
   filled from a lambda, plus a def returning an array. With those,
   `turned(encode(…), yaw)` could be stdlib. Whole-array arithmetic
   (`encode(s1, θ1) + encode(s2, θ2)`) would be V, which was turned down.
   So sources are still summed inside the one lambda, and that reads
   fine.
4. **Measured heads and rooms need convolution.** The sphere is a few
   lines because it is physics. A measured HRTF (pinnae, elevation,
   front and back) is a few hundred taps for each direction and ear, which
   is the spectral/convolution gap again. With today's `delay`s it is
   out of reach.
5. **3-D.** In three dimensions a mode is labelled (l, m). Turning about
   the vertical axis is still a turn of each m. Pitch and roll mix the
   m's within each l (Wigner matrices), which a turn doesn't say. On two
   headphone channels without pinna cues elevation is mostly inaudible,
   so the plane of the ears is the honest target on this machine.

## Answers to the handover's questions

- **Does spatial M need a construct?** No. In 2-D the field is a pair
  array, encoding is a turn, turning the head is a turn and hearing is a
  sum of turns, all of which aither has. The head is a delay and
  a shelf.
- **Stdlib defs or the arrays brief first?** `toward`, `heard` and
  `ears` (with the head model) can be stdlib defs now, once item 1 is
  fixed so `ears` needn't be passed K. Encoding and turning as defs wait
  for item 3. Item 2 should become an error whatever is decided.
- **Stepping:** not touched. Spatial modes carry signals and don't step.
  A room's late field held as modes would (it rings and decays), and
  that would meet the stepping question.
