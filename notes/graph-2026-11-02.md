# The signal graph: where the cuts go (2026-11-02)

A design note, to argue before building. It answers the question
notes/perf-2026-10-30.md left waiting (the spectral gap) and decides how
multicore (notes/multicore-2026-10-18.md) goes on from step 2.

## Why step back

Step 2 cuts a voice into tasks by statement: a play, a `let`, a line.
Statements are how a person laid out the file, not a property of the
sound. The same patch written as one long expression would get no
parallelism at all. A language for everyone can't reward one way of
writing.

## The view

A patch is a graph of signals. Every stateful site (a phasor, a filter,
a delay, a `$state`) is a node; every value one reads from another is an
edge. Reading state is an edge one sample back.

The only thing that forces computing a sample at a time is a **loop** in
that graph: a node whose next value depends, through others, on its own.
The loops are the graph's strongly connected components. A node's own
memory (a phasor adding to itself, a filter's last output) is a loop of
one, and costs nothing: the node runs its block in a loop of its own.

Everything else is **feedforward**, and feedforward can be computed a
block at a time without changing a bit. That claim is already tested:
`make blocks` renders every patch a sample at a time and a block of tasks
at a time, and compares every sample (174 of 174 bit-identical).

One idea, found by the compiler rather than written by the author, then
answers four questions we have been treating apart:

- **Multicore.** Loops that don't feed each other run on different
  cores; a chain of them is a pipeline.
- **SIMD.** Many instances of one loop (`sum(64, …)`'s partials, `poly`'s
  voices, `each`'s spins) are the same code over different state: vector
  lanes, state laid out lane by lane. SIMD across instances, not across
  samples, which aither's recurrences don't allow.
- **The spectral gap.** A block operation (FFT, convolution) is free to
  sit on any feedforward edge: nothing waits on it within the sample.
  Inside a loop it needs a latency of its own (an FFT of 1024 is 1024
  samples late, whatever the engine's block), so the loop hears it late,
  as a delay it can name. Never the engine's block: a patch's sound must
  not depend on AITHER_BUFFER (no machine in patches).
- **Hoisting.** Already a case of it: nodes that change once a block.

## Feedback: near and far

Roland asked whether feedback belongs in aither at all, or whether
patches should be piped into feedback outside it.

Feedback is what aither is. Every oscillator, filter, string, `dho` and
Kuramoto spin is a state that depends on itself, and field physics is
coupled equations. Taking it out would take out the language.

But there are two kinds, and they cost differently:

- **Near:** a loop inside one body, a string's own state, a filter's
  memory. Tight, cheap, sample by sample, and one task: no loss.
- **Far:** coupling between bodies. A reverb fed back into the synth, one
  instrument pulling another. This is what joins two loops into one and
  stops them running apart.

Physics already has the answer for far coupling: across a distance it
takes time. 2.7 ms is sound crossing 0.9 m. If far coupling carries a
delay at least as long as a scheduling step, every body runs on its own
core, and coupling between instruments becomes what it is in a room:
something with a distance.

Roland's piping idea is that answer from outside: patches as bodies,
their coupling the medium between them. Whether a patch says the
distance in its text (in metres or seconds, never blocks) or it falls
out of piping voices into each other is a language decision. **Open, to
argue.** Near feedback stays exactly as it is either way.

## What carries over from step 2

Everything except where the cuts go. Tasks that run a block, buffers on
the edges between them, an order that respects needs, the park and gain
buffers, one copy of the code for `tick` and `tick_block`, and `make
blocks` to prove each refinement bit-identical. The graph view moves the
cut lines from statements to loops; each move is checked the same way.

## Grain

A node is often a few instructions; a buffer per edge would cost more
than it saves. The compiler clusters: fuse a feedforward chain into its
consumer, keep a cluster's inner values in registers, cut only where the
work on each side pays for the buffer between them. Statements were a
crude clustering that happened to be free; the graph lets the cost
decide.

## Measure first

Before choosing what comes after step 2:

1. How much of a typical patch's time is inside loops of more than one
   node, how much is feedforward, how much is own-memory nodes.
2. How much is in repeated instances that could share vector lanes.
3. On general shapes, not three of Roland's sets: a band of plays, a
   bank of partials, a polyphonic keyboard, a coupled field, an effect
   chain.

That decides whether lanes or threads pay first.
