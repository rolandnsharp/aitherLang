# The law apart from the stepping

**Status (2026-10-29): an open design question, nothing built.** Item 3
of "What aither would need" in [fields-2026-10-29.md](fields-2026-10-29.md),
now with evidence. Design first: rewrite real patches both ways before
any syntax is chosen.

## The problem

Every stateful thing in aither is a hand-written integrator: the law of
what changes and the arithmetic of stepping it are one text. A filter's
recurrence, ridge's `2 * c - p + 0.25 * lap`, `dho`'s impulse-invariant
rotation, every `$x = $x + … * dt` in a patch. Physics writes the law
(u'' = c²∇²u, ẋ = v, v̇ = −ω²x − 2γv + F) and chooses how to step it
separately; aither makes the patch do both, and the patch gets the
stepping wrong in ways the law doesn't show.

## The evidence (the gong, same day)

- A first-order step (the push at the start of the sample) on a coupled
  field of modes gained energy as the coupling squared: at a coupling of
  100 a hard strike ran away in 0.2 s. Halving the step halved the
  drift, so the step was at fault, not the law, which conserves
  momentum exactly on paper.
- A midpoint step held momentum to 0.04% in 2 s; Adams–Bashforth (1.5 of
  the push now less 0.5 of the last) to 2% in 3 s at half the cost.
  Each was written by hand into the patch, with the frame conversions it
  needs (the last push turned on by a sample's spin).
- `dho` already is the answer for one linear mode: its exact rotation is
  an exponential integrator, which is why it doesn't drift at
  resonance (PHASORS.md, "The push").

## The idea

The patch says the rate; the engine steps it.

```
$x' = $v                                  # sketch only: the rate of each state
$v' = -w^2 * $x - 2 * d * $v + force
```

with the step chosen by name where it matters (exact for the linear part,
midpoint, Adams–Bashforth, symplectic, implicit), and a default that is
safe. The linear part of a law stepped exactly, the rest on top (the
interaction picture, as the gong did by hand) would make `dho` the first
case of the rule instead of a special builtin.

## Questions to answer before any syntax

1. **How the rate is written.** A `'` on state, a `rate` block, or a def
   returning the rate. How it reads in a patch with ten states.
2. **Pairs and phasors.** A phasor's rate is a turn: the exact step of
   `z' = iωz` is a rotation. Does the rule see that, so spins stay exact?
3. **Coupling across copies and `each`.** A field of modes is many rates
   read together; every rate must see the others at the same instant
   (held-back stores already do this for one step; a multi-stage step
   needs it per stage).
4. **Nonlinear and implicit.** Virtual analog (a ladder filter, a diode
   clipper) wants an implicit solve per sample (Newton, a few
   iterations). Is that the same rule, or a different one?
5. **Cost under TCC.** A two-stage step doubles the law's cost. The
   compiler changes of 2026-10-29 (folded lets, indexes, ifs, no zero
   terms) made coupled fields cheap enough on mains; on battery (four
   1 GHz cores) the gong was still 2.4× too slow.
6. **No machine in patches.** Every state still advances every sample;
   no control rate, no `slow()`. The step changes how, not how often.

## How to test it

Rewrite three real patches both ways, by hand first, in the note:

- a `dho`-based instrument (the linear case: must stay bit-exact or
  better),
- a coupled field (the gong's modes; measure the conserved quantity),
- a nonlinear circuit (a ladder or a clipper from `lib/analogue.aither`).

Judge on reading and on measurements (drift, cost), then choose.
