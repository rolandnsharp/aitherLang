# The ladder: one rule for numbers, pairs and pairs of pairs

**Status (2026-11-06): a design note, nothing built.** Decided with
Roland in discussion: the rule below, `*` as `turn`, and accepting that
`stereo + mono` needs a word. It answers open question 1 of
[nested-pair-2026-11-05.md](nested-pair-2026-11-05.md), replaces that
note's "side by side" arithmetic, and revises PHILOSOPHY.

**Superseded in part (2026-11-06) by
[places-and-values-2026-11-06.md](places-and-values-2026-11-06.md).** The
rule for values and `*` as `turn` stand, and `^` is decided there. Its
"Stereo" section, `both`, and the sentence below don't: stereo became
two places, with its own bracket.

> **A pair is a complex number, and stereo is how you hear it.**

## The rule

A number, a pair and a pair of pairs are real, complex and Hamilton's
numbers: each rung is the one below doubled (Cayley–Dickson). **Whatever
is narrower sits inside the wider, the rest zero, and `+ - * /` are the
algebra's at every rung.**

    number       r          sits in a pair as        [r, 0]
    pair         z          sits in a pair of pairs as  [z, [0, 0]]

The result is as wide as the wider operand. There is nothing else to
remember: no reading to choose, no spreading, no stand-in error for a
pair meeting a pair of pairs.

| written | today | on the ladder |
|---|---|---|
| `z * 0.5`, `q * 0.5` | scales | scales (the same) |
| `a + b`, same shapes | adds | adds (the same) |
| `z + 1` | 1 on both sides | 1 on the real part: `[a + 1, b]` |
| `q + 1` | 1 on all four | 1 on the scalar part, as Hamilton writes it |
| `z * w` | side by side | the complex product (today's `turn`) |
| `z / w` | side by side | `z conj(w) / |w|²` (today's `unturn`) |
| `q * r` | side by side | Hamilton's product; order matters |
| `q + z`, `q * z` | stand-in error | `z` sits inside: `[z, [0, 0]]` |

`turn(q, z)` was the case that looked odd, `(az, b conj(z))`. It isn't an
exception: it is order mattering, the same fact as i j = k and j i = −k.

## `*` is `turn`

`turn` and `unturn` go; `*` and `/` are them. Roland likes the word, and
accepted losing it for the symmetry on condition the docs say why. So
SPEC, COMPOSING and PHILOSOPHY keep the word as the explanation:
**multiplying is turning**. A pair of size 1 turns what it multiplies by
its angle, and scales by its size if it has one; multiplying spins adds
their speeds. That is Steinmetz's operator and Hamilton's versor, and
the reason the word was ever chosen.

```
spin(220) * spin(50)                  # was turn(spin(220), spin(50)): spin(270)
$z = $z * step + [0, -force * dt / wd]
def about(dir, angle): cos(angle / 2) + dir * sin(angle / 2)     # as Hamilton writes it
def apply(r, v): r * v * conj(r)
```

The units get simpler too: `let i = [0, 1]` is enough at both rungs, as
it sits inside a pair of pairs by the rule; `j` and `k` are pairs of
pairs. `w + x * i + y * j + z * k` builds a quaternion, and `1 + 2 * i` a
complex number.

## Why

- **Symmetry.** The same laws at every rung, so working by hand is safe:
  `z * (w + 1)` is `z * w + z`, which spreading breaks.
- **Radio and physics.** A real signal added to an IQ stream belongs on I
  alone; spreading it onto Q is wrong. So for a Steinmetz phasor.
- **Hamilton's formulas read as written.** Real numbers are his scalars,
  and `q + 1` is what it is in his algebra (Hamilton 1846).
- **It stops where the algebra does.** Real, complex and quaternion are
  the rungs that keep associativity, and the next, the octonions, loses
  it (Conway and Smith 2003), which is why a pair of pairs of pairs is
  an error.
- **Fewer words:** `turn` and `unturn` go, and with them the stand-in
  errors for mixed shapes.

## Stereo

Keep the identity of pair and stereo. It is aither's power: any pair can
be heard as two speakers, drawn as the scope's x and y, or sent as I and
Q, which is why a turning phasor is a circle and a sound at once. The
ambiguity was never there; it was in arithmetic having to guess, and the
ladder takes that away.

Two unrelated sounds aren't physically a complex number, but what is
done to a mix doesn't notice: adding, scaling by a number, filtering and
shaping each number are the same either way. Only two things change:

- **Mono into stereo needs a word.** `def both(x): [x, x]`, and
  `stereo + both(mono)`. `stereo + mono` is legal and lands on the left
  alone, **with no error**. This is the design's real price, accepted by
  Roland: it is the commonest move in audio, and forgetting is silent.
- **A gain a side is no longer `*`.** `sig * [0.8, 0.6]` (documented in
  SPEC today) becomes a complex product. It is written by its sides,
  `let [l, r] = sig` then `[l * 0.8, r * 0.6]`. `mono * [gl, gr]` still
  works, a number scaling a pair.

## What doesn't change

- **Functions run once a number** (`tanh`, `sin`, filters, delays), each
  run its own state. That is a different idea from arithmetic, and what
  sound wants. A number given as a parameter is the same on each run. A
  function given a pair of pairs and a pair stays an error: the rule is
  arithmetic's, and a cutoff of 0 on two of four filters means nothing.
- **Comparisons** take numbers.
- **`conj`, `amplitude`, `phase`, `axis`**, the ladder's own readers.
- **The edge:** a pair of pairs reaching the output stays a stand-in
  error (open question 2 of the nested-pair note is not answered here).

**One wart, kept knowingly:** a patch ending in a number plays on both
speakers, though in the algebra that number equals `[x, 0]`, which would
play on the left. The edge is where stereo is heard, so it may have its
own convenience, but `x` and `x + [0, 0]` will sound different.

## PHILOSOPHY, revised

"Complex numbers as operations, not types" says the language takes no
position on which structure of ℂ it means, and that two cells become a
stereo pair or a complex number by which operation reads them. Partly
that stands: there is still no new type, and hearing, the scope and MIDI
are operations reading two cells. But the arithmetic now takes a
position: `+ - * /` mean the algebra, for all pairs. PHILOSOPHY has to
say so rather than be quietly contradicted. Its other cases (a (pitch,
gate) tuple) are named by destructuring, not by arithmetic.

## Open

1. **`^`.** The rule covers `+ - * /`. Should `z ^ 2` be `z * z`? The
   ladder says yes for whole powers; `pow` then stays per number, as a
   function. `mod` stays per number.
2. **Where else a number fills a pair today.** The rule says it should
   sit inside: pair state given a number (`$z = 1`), an array element, an
   `if` with a pair on one branch and a number on the other, a mixed
   `sum`, a def's pair parameter given a number. Probably all `[r, 0]`,
   but these weren't measured (below), and each silently changes.
3. **`both`'s name.** `around.aither` has a local `both`, which is fine.

## Migration

Past patches don't decide the language; they get changed to it. Measured
at 01b6042 by logging every operator in a throwaway compiler over the
195 tracked patches (all compiled), defs inlined so each site as it
compiles:

- `pair * pair`, `pair / pair`: **0**. No def splits on two pairs either
  (a `*` inside one would hide from that count).
- A pair and a number in `+`: **16 sites**, all mono mixed into stereo:
  small (shapes), becoming (3), bell (3), complex_pad, contour,
  freq_shifter, gaelic_ladder, native, orrery, phi_bush, quest, tesla_jam.
  Each takes `both`.
- `turn` and `unturn` calls become `*` and `/`, across patches, stdlib
  and docs.
- **Not counted:** open question 2's cases; the five untracked patches
  and the uncommitted knotsong, which are other sessions' work.

The check is strong: every migrated patch should render bit for bit as
it does now, so any change in the corpus is a site missed.
